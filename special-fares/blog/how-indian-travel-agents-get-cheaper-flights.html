How Do Travel Agents in India Get Cheaper Flights Than MakeMyTrip?

7 min read Published 27 Nov 2026 By Voyage-Ed Travels Desk

Every traveller we book has asked a version of this question in the first 10 minutes: "If MakeMyTrip quotes ₹28,000 for Delhi-Dubai and you're offering it for ₹22,000 — where's the catch?"

Short answer: there isn't one. It's the same airline, same aircraft, same cabin, same seat. The ticket is issued through the same IATA system. What's different is the sourcing channel. OTAs like MakeMyTrip, Yatra and Goibibo source from the public GDS (Global Distribution System). Specialist IATA agents source from three additional channels that aren't accessible to OTAs. This piece explains all three.

The question isn't whether agents can price cheaper. It's whether you know which agents actually have access to the three cheaper channels.

What Is the Public GDS — and Why It's the Most Expensive Channel

Airlines publish their fares on the public GDS (Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport). That's the fare an OTA like MakeMyTrip pulls. It's the published fare the airline wants anyone with a credit card to pay.

The public GDS fare is always higher than wholesale. It has to be — because the airline is paying commissions to intermediaries, absorbing payment processing, and quoting conservatively against last-minute buyers. That's the number you see.

What you don't see is what IATA agents see — three parallel channels where the airline moves seats at NET rates.

The Three Channels That Let Us Price Lower

01

Blocked Inventory (Hard Blocks)

The airline pre-sells seat blocks — 20, 50, 200 seats on a specific sector — to IATA agents and consolidators at a NET rate. We commit to the volume up front. The airline locks the seats off the public GDS. We then sell those seats to our customers at a small markup.

Example: Qatar Airways gives us a block of 40 seats on ATQ-YYZ for 26 December at ₹88,000 NET. We markup ₹5,000 and sell at ₹93,000 all-in. MakeMyTrip can't see these seats because they're off the public GDS. Their lowest published fare is ₹1,32,000.

Where the saving comes from: bulk volume commitment + non-refundable fare rules + the airline wanting predictable revenue.

02

Consolidator Contracts

Airlines contract specialist wholesalers (consolidators) to move inventory on specific routes they struggle to fill through the public GDS. The consolidator agrees to sell at a NET rate below the published fare. Those seats live in the consolidator's system and flow to retail agents like us.

This is how Air India Delhi-Bali, ITA Delhi-Rome, Lufthansa Delhi-Calgary are priced at the levels we show on our Blocked Fares page. All consolidator-sourced.

Where the saving comes from: the airline optimising load factor on sectors with volatile demand.

03

Corporate & IT Fares

Airlines publish separate rate sheets for Indian corporates (usually with TCS, Infosys, Reliance etc.), and for IT travellers specifically. These are called "corporate fares" or "IT fares". They're 10-20% below the public GDS.

IATA agents with corporate relationships can issue these tickets to retail travellers under certain conditions. We use this channel for business travellers flying regularly on the same sector.

Where the saving comes from: volume contracts with the airline + the airline rewarding the consistency.

So Why Doesn't MakeMyTrip Just Get These Fares?

It's a legitimate question and it has three answers:

  • Scale mismatch. OTAs sell millions of tickets a month across every sector. Blocked inventory is 10-200 seats on one sector. It doesn't move the needle at MakeMyTrip scale. They focus on volume at public GDS pricing.
  • Technology constraints. OTA booking engines query the public GDS. Blocked inventory lives in parallel inventory systems (consolidator extranets, airline web agents' portals). OTAs would need separate tech integrations for every consolidator — not worth the engineering lift.
  • Non-refundable fare rules. Blocked inventory comes with strict rules — non-refundable, non-changeable, limited baggage, strict routing. OTAs optimise for self-service — refundable tickets with flexible rules are their default. Blocked fares require human intervention for every booking.

Is a Blocked-Inventory Ticket Safe?

Yes. Fully. This is maybe the most important thing to understand.

A blocked inventory ticket is:

  • Issued through IATA on the same ticket stock as any other ticket
  • On the same aircraft, same cabin, same seat class as a published-fare ticket
  • Covered by the same airline operating conditions and insurance
  • Delivered as an e-ticket with PNR and flight confirmation within 30 minutes

The only differences are the fare rules in the back-end. Non-refundable. Non-changeable. The traveller doesn't see any difference at the airport or on board.

The Catch (Because There Is One)

Blocked fares are:

  • Non-refundable — if you cancel, you lose the ticket cost (minus some taxes)
  • Non-changeable — you can't modify date, route, or passenger name once issued
  • Time-limited — the airline holds the block for 25-30 days then releases unsold seats back to public GDS

This is a trade-off, not a scam. For travellers with firm dates — honeymoon, wedding, visa-stamped travel, PR landing, student return — the trade-off is clearly worth ₹5,000-₹25,000 per ticket. For travellers with uncertain plans, pay the extra ₹8,000-₹12,000 for a flexible fare.

How to Tell If You're Getting a Real Blocked Fare

A few signals when you're shopping around:

  • The agent shows you specific inventory. "Qatar Airways ATQ-YYZ, 26 December, ₹88,000 NET." Not a vague "we'll check with the airline." Specific inventory = real blocked fare.
  • The rate is 10-25% below MakeMyTrip. Larger gaps are suspicious. Smaller gaps are just regular public fares relabelled.
  • They issue immediately with PNR. You get an actual e-ticket and PNR within 30 minutes. Not "we'll confirm later."
  • They give you a GST invoice. This is the biggest marker of a legitimate IATA agent. Fake agents can't issue proper GST invoices.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can a travel agent give a cheaper flight than MakeMyTrip?
Three channels: blocked inventory (airline pre-sells bulk seats at NET), consolidator contracts (quarterly wholesale deals), and corporate/IT fares (volume rates). All three run 5-25% below public GDS pricing.
Is a blocked inventory flight ticket safe?
Yes. It's a regular IATA-issued airline ticket on the same aircraft, same cabin, same seat class, with the same insurance and e-ticket. The only differences are the fare rules — usually non-refundable and non-changeable.
Can I get a GST invoice on a blocked-inventory ticket?
Yes. Every IATA-registered Indian travel agent issues a proper GST invoice (CGST + SGST or IGST @ 18% on the agent's margin). You can claim this against your business GST.
Why don't OTAs like MakeMyTrip show these fares?
OTAs query the public GDS (Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport). Blocked inventory lives outside the public GDS in the agent-wholesaler system. OTAs don't have the contracts or the tech to surface these fares.
Can I cancel a blocked-inventory ticket?
Usually no. Blocked fares are non-refundable and non-changeable. If you need flexibility, we quote a refundable public fare separately — usually ₹8,000-₹12,000 higher.
